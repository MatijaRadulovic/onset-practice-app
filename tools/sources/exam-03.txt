== Penguins
Penguins are fascinating birds that cannot fly, but they are excellent swimmers. Their wings have developed into strong flippers, which allow them to move through the water at great speed while hunting for fish. Most species live in the southern half of the world, but only a few actually spend their lives on the ice of Antarctica. To survive the freezing weather, emperor penguins stand close together in huge groups and take turns standing on the outside, where it is coldest. Scientists are worried because melting ice is making life harder for these birds.

== The Printing Press
The invention of the printing press around 1450 was one of the most important events in European history. In the past, every book had to be copied by hand, which took months and made books so expensive that only churches and kings could buy them. Around 1440, Johannes Gutenberg developed a system of movable metal letters that could be arranged, covered with ink and used again and again. Within fifty years, millions of books had been printed across Europe. Ideas now spread much faster than ever, and ordinary people began to learn to read.

== Working from Home
Working from home has become normal for millions of employees in recent years. Many of them like the freedom to plan their own day and to save the time and money they would otherwise spend on commuting to the office. On the other hand, some people find it hard to separate their private lives from their jobs, and they often feel lonely without colleagues around them. Companies have noticed that spontaneous conversations in the corridor can produce good ideas. Therefore, many employers now offer a mixture of days at home and days in the office.

== Venice
Venice is a unique city built on more than a hundred small islands in a lagoon in northern Italy. There are no cars in the historic old town, so people move around on foot or by boat, and goods are delivered along narrow canals. Because the buildings stand on wooden piles driven deep into the mud, they have survived for several centuries. Unfortunately, the city is slowly sinking, and high water now floods the streets several times a year. At the same time, the huge number of tourists makes life difficult for local residents.

== Sugar and Health
Most people know that too much sugar is bad for their health, but few realise how much they actually eat. Sugar is not only found in cakes and sweets, because manufacturers also add large amounts to bread, sauces and even products that are advertised as healthy. A high intake can cause tooth decay, weight gain and, in the long term, serious illnesses such as diabetes. Doctors therefore advise people to read the list of ingredients carefully before buying food. Replacing sugary drinks with water is a simple change that can make a big difference.

== Mount Everest
Mount Everest, the highest mountain on Earth, has fascinated adventurers for more than a century. It rises nine thousand metres above sea level, and on the summit the air contains about a third of the oxygen found at the coast. Climbers therefore need weeks to become used to high altitude before they try to reach the top. In 1953, Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay became the first people to stand on top. Since then, thousands have followed, and the growing crowds have caused new problems such as long queues and rubbish.

== Fast Fashion
Fast fashion describes clothes that are produced quickly and cheaply to follow the latest trends. Shops bring out new collections every few weeks, and customers are tempted to buy far more than they really need. Many of these clothes are worn only a few times before they are thrown away. The industry also uses huge amounts of water and chemicals, and workers in some countries receive very low wages. More and more consumers are therefore turning to second-hand shops.

== Street Art
Street art has grown from an illegal activity into a respected form of modern art. In the beginning, young people secretly sprayed their names on walls and trains at night, and the police treated them as criminals. Today, many cities invite talented painters to decorate buildings, because bright pictures can make a dull area more attractive. Some artists have become very famous, and their works are sold for huge sums at auctions. Critics point out that the rebellious spirit may disappear when a company pays for the work.
