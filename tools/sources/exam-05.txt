== Pizza
Pizza is probably the best known Italian dish, and people eat it in almost every country. Flat breads with toppings have existed since ancient times, but the modern version with tomatoes and cheese appeared in Naples in the eighteenth century. At first, poor workers ate it in the street because it was cheap. Only after Italian emigrants opened restaurants in America did pizza become fashionable abroad. Today you can order it with hundreds of different toppings.

== The Moon Landing
On 20 July 1969, millions of people sat in front of their televisions to watch one of the greatest moments in history. Neil Armstrong climbed out of the lunar module and became the first human being to walk on the surface of the Moon. His colleague Buzz Aldrin joined him minutes later, while a third astronaut waited in orbit above them. The two men picked up rocks, took photographs and planted a flag before flying back safely to Earth.

== Dolphins
Dolphins are intelligent animals that have fascinated people for centuries, and scientists still study them today. They live in groups, called pods, and talk with each other using a complex system of clicks and whistles. Experts have discovered that every dolphin has its own whistle, which works almost like a name. They also help each other, and there are many reports of dolphins protecting injured members of the pod or even swimmers in danger. Unfortunately, fishing nets, pollution and noise from ships threaten many species.

== Studying Abroad
Studying abroad is an experience that many students remember for the rest of their lives. Living in a foreign country forces young people to become independent, because they have to find a flat, set up a bank account and deal with unfamiliar rules. At the same time, they improve their language skills much faster than in a classroom and meet others from all over the world. Of course, there are difficulties such as homesickness and the high cost of living. Employers usually value this experience, since it shows that a candidate is flexible.

== Wind Energy
Wind energy is one of the fastest growing sources of electricity in the world. Huge turbines turn the movement of the air into power without producing any gases, and the supply will never run out. Large wind farms are often built at sea, where the breeze is stronger and steadier than on land. Critics complain that the machines spoil the view and sometimes hurt birds, but engineers are working on solutions to these problems.

== Chess
Chess is a board game that has been played for around fifteen hundred years. It probably began in India and later came to Europe via Persia, where the rules gradually changed into the modern ones. In a game, two people move their pieces across sixty-four squares, and the aim is to trap the opponent's king. Although it is simple to learn, the number of possible games is almost endless. In 1997, a computer beat the world champion for the first time.

== Airports
Airports have turned into small cities with their own shops, restaurants and even hotels. Every day, thousands of people walk through the terminals, and hundreds of employees work behind the scenes to make sure that everything runs smoothly. Security checks are often the part that people dislike most, because they take time and require you to unpack your bag. Nevertheless, they are considered necessary to keep flights safe. Experts advise arriving at least two hours before an international flight, since long queues and last-minute changes are common.

== Vitamins
Vitamins are substances that the human body needs in small amounts to stay healthy. Most of them cannot be produced by our own cells, so we have to take them in with our food. A lack of vitamin C, for example, causes a serious illness that used to be common among sailors on long voyages. Today, people in rich countries rarely suffer from such shortages, yet many still buy expensive tablets. Doctors say that a varied diet with plenty of fruit and vegetables usually provides everything we need.
