== Bread
Bread is one of the oldest prepared foods in human history and a basic part of meals in most cultures. The first loaves were probably flat and hard, made from crushed grains and water that people baked on hot stones. Later the Egyptians discovered that dough left in a warm place would rise and become light and soft. Different regions have developed their own traditions, from French baguettes to Indian naan and German rye bread. Today many people enjoy baking at home.

== Shakespeare
William Shakespeare is widely regarded as the greatest writer in the English language. He grew up in a small town, but as a young man he became an actor and playwright in London. During his career, he wrote dozens of plays, including the tragedy Hamlet and comedies such as Twelfth Night. Numerous everyday phrases were first used in his works. Four hundred years after his death, his plays are still performed all over the world.

== Video Games
Video games have grown from a small hobby into a gigantic industry that earns more money than the film business. Many parents believe that games are a waste of time and make children aggressive, but research does not clearly support this view. Actually, some games improve logic, reaction speed and teamwork, especially when players cooperate online. Some schools even use them as teaching tools in subjects like history and mathematics. Of course, too many hours in front of a screen are unhealthy.

== Glaciers
Glaciers are huge rivers of ice that move very slowly down mountain valleys. They form when snow falls again and again over many winters and is gradually pressed into solid ice by its own weight. Over thousands of years, they have shaped the landscape by carving channels and lakes. Today most of them are shrinking because of rising temperatures. This is a serious problem for millions of people, as the melting water supplies rivers that provide drinking water.

== The Bicycle
The bicycle, in its modern form, is an invention that has hardly changed for over a century. The first versions had no pedals, and riders had to push themselves along the ground with their feet. Later designers added pedals, a chain and rubber wheels, and suddenly cycling became easy and fast. The bicycle also gave ordinary people more freedom than ever before, especially women, who could now travel alone. Today there are more than a billion bicycles in the world.

== Volunteering
Volunteering means giving your time to help others without receiving any payment. Millions of people do this regularly, for example by working in animal shelters, visiting lonely seniors or coaching children at a sports club. Many volunteers say that they gain at least as much as they give, because they learn new skills and make friends. Employers often value such experience highly since it shows initiative and responsibility. You do not have to travel far to find a good cause.

== Earthquakes
Earthquakes happen when large pieces of the Earth's crust suddenly move against each other. Most are barely noticeable, but some are strong enough to destroy whole cities within seconds. Regions where the plates meet, such as Japan or California, are dangerous. Scientists cannot predict the exact time of a quake, although they can calculate which areas are at risk. Engineers have therefore designed buildings that can sway without collapsing.

== Human Memory
Human memory is far less reliable than most people think, and it often changes without us noticing. We tend to believe that it works like a camera, storing perfect copies of past events, but in reality the brain rebuilds every memory each time we recall it. Many people who experienced the same situation often remember it quite differently. This is a serious problem in court, where the statements of witnesses can decide the fate of a defendant. Psychologists recommend repeating information at regular intervals.
