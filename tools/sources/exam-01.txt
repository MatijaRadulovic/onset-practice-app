== Coffee
Coffee is one of the most popular drinks in the world, and legend says that it was found by a goat herder in Africa. One day, he noticed that his animals were very lively after they had eaten the berries of a small bush. In the end, he tried them himself and felt wide awake. Monks heard of the discovery and began to make a hot drink from the beans, which helped them to stay awake during long evening prayers.

== Sleep
Sleep is much more important for our health than most people realise. At night, the body repairs damaged cells, the brain sorts the memories of the day and the immune system becomes stronger. Experts say that adults need seven to nine hours of sleep, but many people have far less. Over time, this can lead to weight gain, poor concentration and even heart disease. A regular bedtime is one of the simplest ways to improve your sleep.

== The Great Wall of China
The Great Wall of China is one of the most impressive structures ever made by human hands. It is not a single wall, but a series of walls and towers that different rulers built over many centuries. Their aim was to protect the northern borders of the empire from attacks by tribes on horseback. Thousands of workers carried stones and bricks across steep mountains, and many of them never returned home. Today, millions of tourists visit the best preserved sections every year.

== Bees
Bees are among the most useful insects on our planet, although many people are afraid of them. As well as producing honey, they help a large number of plants to grow fruit, so much of our food depends on their work. When a bee visits a flower to collect nectar, tiny grains of pollen stick to its body and are carried to the next one. Sadly, many have died in recent years because of pesticides and the loss of wild meadows.

== Public Libraries
Public libraries have changed a lot in recent years, but they are still very much alive. Although many people now read novels on their phones, such places offer much more than shelves full of books. Visitors can use free computers, join language courses or simply find a quiet place to study. In small towns, the library is often the only cultural centre that local people have. Many also organise reading programmes for children, which help them to discover the joy of stories.

== Cycling in Cities
More and more people in big cities are choosing to travel by bicycle. The reasons are easy to understand: a bike is cheap, quiet and does not pollute the air. In a crowded centre, cyclists are often quicker than drivers, who spend ages sitting in traffic and looking for a parking space. Some cities, such as Copenhagen, have built special lanes to make riding safer. Doctors also welcome the change, since regular exercise keeps people healthy.

== The Invention of the Telephone
In 1876, Alexander Graham Bell received a patent for a machine that would change communication for ever. He had spent years trying to find a way to send the human voice along a wire, partly because he taught deaf children. The first message to his assistant in the next room was very short, but it proved that the idea worked. Within a few years, thousands of telephones were installed in homes and offices across the United States.

== Learning Languages
Learning a foreign language is a challenge that can bring great rewards. Many adults think they are too old to start, but studies show that motivation and regular practice matter much more than age. The best way is to use the language as often as possible, for example by watching films, listening to songs or talking to native speakers. Making mistakes is a normal part of learning, and nobody should feel embarrassed about it.
