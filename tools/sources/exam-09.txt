== Rice
Rice is the main food for more than half of the world's population. It grows best in warm and wet regions, which is why most of it is produced in Asia, where whole villages take part in the harvest. In flooded fields, called paddies, workers push the young plants into the mud by hand, and they need plenty of water. There are thousands of varieties, from the long white grains used for everyday meals to the sticky type used in sushi. Because rice is cheap and easy to store, it feeds enormous populations.

== The Silk Road
The Silk Road was not a single road but a network of trade routes that connected China with the Mediterranean for more than a thousand years. Merchants journeyed with camels across deserts and mountains, and few of them covered the whole distance themselves. Instead, goods were passed from trader to trader, and each of them made a profit. Besides silk, the caravans carried spices, precious stones, paper and tea. Ideas and religions also moved along these routes, and diseases spread in the same way.

== Smartphones
Smartphones have become so important in our daily lives that many people feel uncomfortable when they leave home without one. These tiny devices combine a telephone, a camera, a map, a music player and a computer, and billions use them. Most of us check our screens dozens of times a day, often for no particular reason. Experts are worried that this habit makes it harder to concentrate and to enjoy real conversations. Several schools have therefore banned them during lessons.

== Wolves
Wolves have always played a powerful role in human imagination, and they appear as villains in countless stories. In reality, these animals are shy and avoid contact with people if they can. They live in family groups, called packs, which hunt together and take care of the young. Because farmers feared for their sheep, wolves were hunted until they had almost disappeared from large parts of Europe. In recent decades, however, protection laws have allowed them to return to several countries.

== Public Transport
Public transport is essential for the functioning of any large and crowded city. Buses, trams and trains can carry many more passengers than private cars, and they take up far less space on the road. Cities with a reliable system suffer less from traffic jams and air pollution, and their residents often save a lot of money. The most common complaints are delays, overcrowded vehicles and high ticket prices. Some cities have tried to solve these problems by offering a cheap yearly pass.

== Tattoos
Tattoos have a much longer history than most people assume, and they have meant different things in different cultures. Scientists found drawings on the skin of a man who died more than five thousand years before our time and was preserved in the ice of the Alps. Often, tattoos were used to show membership of a tribe, to mark important events or to protect the wearer from evil spirits. In Europe, they were associated for a long time with sailors and criminals, but today they are a common fashion.

== Lighthouses
Lighthouses have guided sailors safely through dangerous waters for more than two thousand years. The most famous one in antiquity towered over the port of Alexandria and was counted among the Seven Wonders of the World. For centuries, keepers lit fires at the top of the tower each evening and kept them burning until dawn. Later, powerful lamps and glass lenses sent a bright beam far across the sea. Today satellites and radar have made most lighthouses unnecessary, but many are preserved as monuments.

== Yoga
Yoga is an ancient practice that originated in India and is now followed by millions of people around the globe. It combines gentle stretching with breathing techniques and periods of quiet reflection. Many beginners imagine that you have to be extremely flexible, but in fact the postures can be adapted to every level. Regular practice may reduce back pain, lower stress and help people to sleep better. Classes are offered in gyms, parks and even offices, and teachers stress that you should never force your body into a painful position.
