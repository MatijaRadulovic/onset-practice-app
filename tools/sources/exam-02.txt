== Chocolate
Chocolate has a long and surprising history that begins in Central America. The Maya and the Aztecs never ate it as a sweet, but drank it as a bitter liquid made from roasted cocoa beans. When Spanish explorers brought the beans back to Europe in the sixteenth century, they added sugar to make the drink more pleasant. For a long time, chocolate was an expensive luxury that only rich families could afford. New machines finally made cheap bars possible in the nineteenth century.

== Volcanoes
Volcanoes are among the most powerful and dangerous forces of nature, yet they also fascinate many people. They form where hot liquid rock, called magma, rises from deep inside the Earth and breaks through the surface. Some volcanoes erupt almost constantly, while others stay quiet for hundreds of years and then explode without warning. The ash from a large eruption can travel thousands of kilometres and even stop aeroplanes from flying. Despite the danger, millions of people live near volcanoes because the soil there is very good for farming.

== Social Media
Social media has totally changed the way we communicate with each other. Years ago, we had to call friends or write letters to share our news, but today a single photo can reach hundreds of people within seconds. This is certainly convenient, but it also has disadvantages. Many users compare their own lives with the perfect pictures of others and end up feeling unhappy. Experts therefore advise us to use these platforms carefully and to take regular breaks.

== The Titanic
The Titanic was the largest passenger ship in the world when it left Southampton in April 1912. Its owners called it unsinkable, because it had a system of separate sections. Only four days later, however, the liner hit an iceberg in the North Atlantic and began to sink. There were not enough lifeboats for all the people on board, and more than fifteen hundred people lost their lives. After the disaster, new rules were introduced to make ships safer.

== Recycling
Recycling is one of the easiest ways for ordinary people to protect the environment. Instead of throwing away used paper, glass and plastic, we can collect these materials so that factories can turn them into new products. Recycling saves energy and reduces the amount of rubbish that ends up in landfills. In some countries, shops even charge a small deposit on bottles, which customers get back when they return them. However, recycling alone is not enough, and we must also buy less.

== Marathon Running
Marathon running has become a popular hobby for thousands of ordinary people, not just professional athletes. The distance of twenty-six miles sounds scary, but with preparation almost any healthy person can finish it. Most training plans last about four months and increase the length of the weekly runs. Beginners often start too fast, which leaves them exhausted long before the finish line. Many runners say that the atmosphere is the best part, because crowds along the route cheer for every participant.

== Tea Culture in Britain
Tea is so closely connected with British life that many people see it as a national symbol. In fact, the habit only arrived in England in the 1600s, and at first it was far too expensive for most families. Over the next hundred years, trade with India and China made it cheaper, and it soon became the most popular drink of all social classes. The tradition of afternoon tea is said to have started with a duchess who felt hungry between lunch and dinner.

== Space Exploration
Space exploration has produced many discoveries that make our everyday lives on Earth easier. Satellites let us forecast the weather, find our way and watch television shows from all over the world. Scientists have developed new materials and medical devices while preparing astronauts for their missions. However, critics argue that space projects are far too expensive and that the money could be spent on more urgent problems such as hunger or disease. Supporters reply that curiosity has always driven human progress.
