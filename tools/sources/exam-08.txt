== Bananas
Bananas are the most popular fruit in the world, and millions of people eat them every day. They grow in warm countries, mainly in Latin America, Asia and Africa, and ships carry them to Europe before they are ripe. Once they arrive, they are stored in special rooms where a gas makes them turn yellow. A banana is a good source of energy and contains a lot of potassium, which is important for the muscles. Scientists worry that one disease could destroy most plantations, because almost all bananas are genetically identical.

== Leonardo da Vinci
Leonardo da Vinci was one of the most talented people who ever lived. Born in a village near Florence in 1452, he became famous as a painter, but he was also an inventor, an engineer and a scientist. His notebooks are full of drawings of flying machines, bridges and even an early type of tank, most of which were never built in his lifetime. He studied the human body carefully and made pictures of muscles and bones. The Mona Lisa, which he painted over several years, is probably the most visited painting in the world.

== Noise in Cities
Noise is a problem that most city residents have learned to accept, although it affects their health more than they realise. Traffic, building sites and aircraft produce a constant din that hardly ever stops, even at night. Studies show that people living in noisy districts are more likely to suffer from sleep problems, stress and high blood pressure. Some cities have therefore introduced lower speed limits and quieter roads. Trees also help, because they absorb noise and give residents a place to relax.

== Octopuses
Octopuses are some of the most remarkable and intelligent creatures in the ocean. They have eight arms, three hearts and blue blood, and they can change their skin pattern within a split second to hide from enemies. In tests, they have shown that they can solve puzzles, open jars and even escape from closed tanks at night. Scientists are amazed because these animals have no bones and live for only a few years, which makes their intelligence all the more surprising. Most of their neurons are located in their arms.

== Nursing
Nursing is a demanding profession that requires patience, physical strength and a great deal of knowledge. Nurses do much more than give medicine and change bandages, since they also observe patients carefully, talk to worried relatives and often notice problems before a doctor does. Shifts are long and frequently include nights, weekends and public holidays. In many countries there is a serious shortage of qualified staff, so those who work in hospitals have to cope with enormous pressure. Better pay and shorter hours could attract more young people.

== Carnival
Carnival is a festival that is celebrated in many parts of the world shortly before the Christian period of fasting begins. People dress in bright costumes, wear masks and take to the streets to enjoy music, dance and parades. The most famous event takes place in Brazil, where dance groups prepare for a whole year to join the spectacular procession. In Europe, cities such as Venice and Cologne have their own traditions, which differ greatly from one another. Whatever the local custom, for a few days ordinary rules are forgotten.

== Skyscrapers
Skyscrapers have changed the appearance of cities since the end of the nineteenth century. The first tall buildings were built in American cities such as Chicago, where land was expensive and architects had to build upwards to make a profit. Two inventions made it possible: a steel frame that could carry enormous weight, and a safe elevator that saved people from climbing hundreds of stairs. Today the tallest tower, in Dubai, is more than eight hundred metres high. Critics argue that such giants use huge amounts of energy.

== Fairy Tales
Fairy tales are stories that have been told for hundreds of years, and they still fascinate children and adults alike. At first, most were not written down, but passed on by word of mouth. In the nineteenth century, two brothers from Germany collected dozens of these stories from villagers and published them in a famous book. Original versions were often much darker than the ones we know today, with cruel punishments and frightening endings. Experts believe that such tales help children to deal with their fears.
