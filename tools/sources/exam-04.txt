== Honey
Honey is a natural food that people have valued for thousands of years. Bees make it from the nectar of flowers, which they store in the wax cells of their hive and thicken with their wings. Its taste and appearance depend on the plants that the bees have visited, so clover honey is light and mild, while forest honey is dark and strong. Jars of honey found in ancient Egyptian tombs were still perfectly edible after three thousand years.

== The Football World Cup
The Football World Cup is the most watched sporting event on the planet, followed by billions of people. The first tournament took place in Uruguay in 1930, but only thirteen teams came because the journey across the ocean was long and expensive. Since then, the contest has been held every four years, apart from during the Second World War. Today, countries compete hard to qualify, and the host nation spends huge amounts of money on new stadiums. For many fans, the best moments are the surprises, when a small team beats one of the favourites.

== Electric Cars
Electric cars are becoming more common on our roads, and their sales are rising every year. They produce no exhaust gases at all, and they are much quieter than petrol engines, which makes cities more pleasant for people on foot. The biggest worry of buyers used to be the short range of the batteries, but newer cars can travel several hundred miles on a single charge. However, there are still not enough charging stations in many areas, and the cars are more expensive than similar models.

== Time Zones
Time zones are a fairly recent invention that we now take for granted. Until the nineteenth century, every town set its clocks by the sun, which caused no problems as long as people went slowly on horses. The arrival of the railway changed this, because timetables became confusing when each station used its own local time. In 1884, an international conference chose Greenwich in London as the starting point for the twenty-four zones of the world. Russia now has eleven time zones, while China uses just one.

== Museums
Museums have become much more exciting places than they were a generation ago. In the past, visitors walked slowly past glass cases and read long descriptions, and many children found the experience boring. Today, most large museums offer displays where you can touch objects, play games or even try on historical costumes. Some also let guests explore the collection on their phones before they arrive. Although tickets can be expensive, many cities have free days or discounts for students.

== Hurricanes
Hurricanes are enormous storms that form over warm ocean water near the equator. The heat makes large amounts of moist air rise, and as it climbs, it starts to spin because of the rotation of the Earth. Once the winds reach a speed of seventy-four miles per hour, the system is officially called a hurricane. They cause heavy rain, flooding and great damage along the coast, but they lose their strength quickly once they move over land. Modern satellites allow experts to warn local residents several days in advance.

== Online Shopping
Online shopping has changed the habits of consumers all over the world. Instead of visiting several shops, you can check prices at home, read reviews by other customers and have the goods delivered to your door. This is especially helpful for people who live far from big cities or find it hard to carry heavy bags. The downside is that many small local stores cannot compete with the low prices of huge internet companies and are forced to close.

== The Amazon Rainforest
The Amazon rainforest is the largest tropical forest on Earth and a home to millions of species. It covers an area in South America that is almost as big as Europe, and it contains about one tenth of all known animals and plants. Its trees absorb enormous amounts of carbon dioxide, which is why the jungle is called the lungs of the planet. Sadly, large parts are cut down every year to make room for farms and cattle.
